**Your Life Insurance — the main focus**

You hold a single life policy with **AIA**, taken out in **2014**, insuring you for approximately **$915,000** at a cost of around **$5,600 a year** — **$467 a month**. That bill is what prompted the call, and rightly so.

The reason it has blown out isn't that you've done anything wrong. Insurers routinely lift the rates on older policies, on the assumption that a long-standing policyholder can't easily move — often because of health, and often because a policy taken out twelve years ago is simply set and forget. Unlike a bank, you can't ring them and negotiate. Add stepped premiums rising with age each year and indexation quietly lifting the sum insured, and this is where it ends up.

**Ownership — super versus your own pocket**

Your AIA policy is structured within the super environment, though you've been funding the premium personally. We talked through both options for the new cover:

- **Inside super**, paid annually, the fund receives a **15% tax deduction on the premium** which is passed back to you — effectively a 15% discount, applied automatically.
- **Outside super**, paid from your own pocket, you don't get that rebate, but the premium doesn't erode your super balance.

Given the premium is now modest — potentially around **$90 a month**, and **$100 to $110 worst case** — my suggestion is that we hold it **personally, outside super**. Michael, you've only got two years of contributions behind you, and at that stage protecting the balance matters more than a 15% saving on a small premium.
