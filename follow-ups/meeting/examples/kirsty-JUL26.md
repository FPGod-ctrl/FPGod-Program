**Superannuation & Investment Strategy**

The heart of our discussion was making your super work harder while staying genuinely conservative — because at your stage, super is simply the most tax-effective place in Australia to hold your money.

- **Consolidation and active management.** Rather than leaving your money in REST in an option you can't easily see, one avenue worth exploring is consolidating your super onto the Colonial First State platform, with the investments managed by us here at Lakeside (myself and Ross). CFS is just the platform — the login and administration, much like Netwealth or Macquarie Wrap; the investment decisions are made by us, not CFS. You'd log in exactly as you do with REST, but with us actively managing the underlying investments and reviewing them with you every six to twelve months.
- **A conservative, diversified approach.** Our fund is effectively a fund of around 10–20 underlying managed funds, so it's well diversified. We only use funds rated highly by an independent research house (Lonsec), each with a long track record and experienced managers — we deliberately favour quality and experience over chasing returns. For your risk profile that realistically targets around **5% p.a.** (typically 4–9% depending on the mix), which suits exactly the hands-off, no-surprises approach you're after.
- **Concessional (before-tax) contributions.** The cap is **$32,500** this financial year, and contributions are tax-deductible. Your employer contributions (12%, rising to 12.5%) use around $8,000 of that, leaving roughly **$24,000 of headroom** you could contribute personally for a deduction — as with the $3,000 you recently added, which reduces your taxable income. This is available while you meet the work test (40 hours in a 30-day period), covering you this financial year and next.
- **Catch-up (carry-forward) contributions.** Because your total super balance is under $500,000, you may also be able to use unused concessional caps from the past five years — potentially a larger deductible contribution. We'd confirm the exact figure first.
- **The flexibility of super.** Because you're past preservation age, moving your ~$100,000 of cash into super does **not** lock it away — you can move into pension phase and access it. What it does is shift those earnings into a tax-free environment targeting ~5%, rather than the ~3.5% you're currently taxed on. In practice, your super and your bank account are now one and the same.

**Wills & Estate Planning**

You were clear you want to keep sole ownership of your home during your lifetime, pass it to your children, and minimise any tax on your passing.

- We discussed a **Will incorporating a testamentary trust** — structured so you keep full ownership and control now, with your assets passing into a protected trust for your children later. This gives strong asset protection (for example, shielding their inheritance in a relationship breakdown) and lets income be distributed among beneficiaries tax-effectively.
- Importantly, your super doesn't automatically follow your Will — it passes via a binding nomination made directly with the fund, which we'll make sure is current.

**Insurance for Your Daughters — Critical**

This is the one area I'd flag as genuinely critical. Both of your daughters now have families and partners who depend on them, and it's important they have appropriate personal insurance in place.

Here's why it matters to your plan too: if either of your daughters (or their partners) couldn't work through illness or injury without cover, the pressure on their household would be immediate — and in that situation it's almost always the parents who step in. That would mean drawing down the very retirement savings we're working to protect. Put simply, them being properly insured protects both their families and your nest egg.

The covers worth reviewing for each of them:

- **Income Protection** — replaces part of their income if they can't work (the most important for a working parent).
- **Life Insurance** — a lump sum to their family in the event of death.
- **TPD** — a lump sum if they're permanently unable to work again.
- **Trauma (Critical Illness)** — a lump sum on diagnosis of a serious condition such as cancer, heart attack or stroke.
