**Increasing your life cover**

You raised increasing your life cover, largely off the back of the new property purchase and the roughly $700,000 of debt attached to it. Your thinking there is exactly right — the question is what's actually achievable.

The honest position, as it was when we last spoke, is that the quadruple bypass in June 2022 makes new fully underwritten life cover very difficult right now. Even then, it will almost certainly come with a loading — typically in the order of 100% to 150%. In plain terms, that means paying roughly double, or more, what the rest of the market pays for the same amount of cover. It's not a reason not to look, but you should know what you'd be signing up for before we go down that road.

**The two realistic options in the meantime**

1. **Accident-only cover.** This can be put in place quickly and very cheaply. The catch is significant: it pays only if death results from an accident. Illness and sickness are excluded, and the overwhelming majority of death claims arise from illness rather than accident — so it covers a small slice of the actual risk. Cheap, easy, and available today if you want it, but I want you going in with clear eyes about what it does and doesn't do.

2. **Let your existing cover index up — my preference for now.** You currently hold **$1,155,000** of life cover. Each renewal that figure increases automatically with CPI, somewhere between 2% and 5% a year. So next year it might sit around $1.22 million, the year after higher again. Those renewal letters are easy to skim past, but that's the mechanism quietly keeping your cover in step with inflation, and it's by far the most cost-effective way to increase your sum insured given your history.

The reason it matters: if one of them became seriously unwell and couldn't work, the bank of Mum and Dad is open for the next forty years. For roughly $1,000 a year you're insuring an earning capacity worth millions over a lifetime. It's the single best-value cover there is at that age, and worth putting on the agenda now.
