**The second thing: the two types of Will**

You asked what I meant by this, so here it is properly.

A **basic Will** is what most people have, and there's nothing wrong with it. Assets pass to the surviving partner, then to the children, in their own names. Simple and effective.

A **Will incorporating a testamentary trust** works differently. Rather than assets landing directly in your children's hands, they drop into a trust that the children control and can use freely. Two reasons that matters:

- **Asset protection.** Assets in the trust aren't held in their personal names. If one of them goes through a relationship breakdown down the track, the estate you've spent a lifetime building isn't on the table to be divided. They can still use it and benefit from it — it just isn't theirs to lose.
- **Tax.** Income from the trust can be streamed to children and grandchildren, and — unusually — grandchildren are taxed at **adult rates** rather than the penalty rates that normally apply to minors. Over time that's a significant saving compared with the same money sitting in an adult child's name at their marginal rate.

The catch is that a testamentary trust can only be created through a Will. It has to be in place beforehand — it can't be arranged afterwards.

**This matters particularly given one of your children is self-employed.** Where someone carries business risk, how assets are passed to them is not a small detail. If a claim or dispute ever arose and a large sum had recently landed in their personal name, it's exposed. Through a trust, it isn't. It's the same money and the same intention — just structured so it can't be taken.
