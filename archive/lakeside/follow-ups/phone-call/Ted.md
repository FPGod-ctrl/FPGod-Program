**Wills & estate planning** — for an estate your size, the key question is whether your Wills are the **basic** kind or a **comprehensive Will incorporating a testamentary trust**. The quick test: a basic Will runs a few pages, a comprehensive one runs 20-plus. The comprehensive version matters for two reasons:

- **Asset protection** — if wealth eventually passes to a child or grandchild who later goes through a relationship breakdown, assets held in the trust are far better shielded. At your level, that protection can be worth a great deal.
- **Tax effectiveness** — distributions to younger family members are taxed at ordinary adult marginal rates rather than the punitive rates that normally apply to children, which is a genuine ongoing saving across a family.

It's also worth checking your **super** is directed sensibly — benefits paid to non-dependent adult children can be taxed on the way through, which catches a lot of people out. None of this is urgent, and you may well have it all in hand — but you've got everything else in order, and I'd hate for this to be the gap. If you send the Wills through, I'm happy to take a look.
