Wills, Estate Planning & Asset Protection

This was one of the biggest areas we covered, and it works in two directions — the wealth coming down to you from your parents, and the wealth you're building that will one day pass on.

- Your parents' estate. Your parents are self-funded retirees in their 60s and will be transitioning soon. With four children and a substantial amount of wealth to flow down, it's critical this is done the right way. My strong suggestion is that your parents have comprehensive (testamentary trust) Wills in place, so that any inheritance flows into a testamentary trust rather than directly into your personal hands.
- Why this matters so much for you specifically. As a company director and shareholder, you carry litigation risk. Worst case: you're in the middle of being sued, an inheritance lands directly in your name at that moment, and it's suddenly up for grabs. Assets received via a testamentary trust sit far more protectively — you inherit "the right way, not the wrong way".

A quick summary of the two Will types we discussed:

  Basic Will — a straightforward document directing that your assets pass directly to your nominated beneficiaries. Simple and cheap, but it offers no ongoing asset protection and no tax flexibility once the assets land in the beneficiary's hands.

  Comprehensive (Testamentary Trust) Will — instead of assets passing directly, they flow into a trust created by the Will on death. The beneficiary controls the trust but the assets are held one step removed, which provides two major advantages: asset protection (assets are far harder to reach in the event of a lawsuit, bankruptcy or relationship breakdown) and tax-effective income streaming (trust income can be distributed across family members, including minors, on more favourable terms). For anyone with business/litigation exposure or meaningful wealth to pass on, these are the gold standard.
