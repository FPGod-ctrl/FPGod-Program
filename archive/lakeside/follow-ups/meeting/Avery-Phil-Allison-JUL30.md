**The longer game — three things to keep in mind**

1. **Sell the investment properties once you're in pension phase.** When the fund is in pension phase there's no tax on earnings and no capital gains tax on sale. That's precisely why the property is held in the fund. Selling while you're still earning is the expensive version of the same transaction.
2. **Transition to retirement.** Phil, being over 60, you can start drawing from super without retiring. One important caveat: the minimum drawdown is a percentage of your balance each year, and the property makes up around 90% of the fund. Before starting a TTR we need to be confident there's enough cash to meet the minimum comfortably — you should never be in a position where a drawdown forces a property sale. Your employer contributions and the fund's cash cover it, but it's a genuine consideration rather than a formality.
3. **The downsizer contribution.** If you ever sold the home and bought something smaller and single level, you can each put **$300,000** into super within 90 days of settlement, entirely outside the normal caps. On the numbers we discussed that would take the fund past $1.6 million — around $80,000 a year tax-free at a conservative 5%, which is the equivalent of a $120,000 salary before tax.
