**Why this contract matters so much**

Yours is a **legacy contract** — written before October 2021. In that month the regulator forced insurers to withdraw this style of policy, because the terms were so favourable to policyholders that the insurers were losing money and none would move first. Two features in particular:

- **The occupation definition.** You can claim if you're unable to perform *one* duty of your occupation. That's a very low bar to get on claim, and it's the single most valuable feature of the contract.
- **Agreed value.** The insurer pays the sum insured regardless of what your income happens to be at the time of claim. For someone self-employed, whose drawings move around with the business, that removes an enormous amount of claim-time argument.

Alongside those you have a **90-day waiting period** (benefits from day 91), a **benefit period to age 65**, and the **needle-stick benefit** built in, covering conditions such as hepatitis B and C contracted at work. Unlikely in your setting, but it costs nothing to hold.

**None of this can be bought today.** The modern replacements are materially inferior, and noticeably so for self-employed professionals. So the guiding principle from here is simple: **protect this policy.** Don't let it lapse, and don't replace it with anything on the current market without talking to me first.
