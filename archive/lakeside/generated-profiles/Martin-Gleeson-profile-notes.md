- Martin has been weighing **winding it up** and rolling into a public fund, mainly to be rid of the work of managing shares

**Miriam — ~$80,000** in an industry fund *(name to confirm — possibly Aware Super or Australian Retirement Trust; Martin was unsure and referred to a recent rebrand)*

**Case for retaining the SMSF:** super does not pass under the Will. Where a death benefit is paid to an **adult, non-dependent child**, the taxable component is taxed at **15% plus Medicare levy**. The usual strategy is to withdraw the benefit before it is paid as a death benefit — and an SMSF can be liquidated in a day or two, where a large public fund cannot. Confirm the tax treatment with the accountant.

**Middle path:** retain the SMSF but move the underlying investments into a **managed portfolio via Colonial First State**, with fund selection and review handled by Lakeside. Removes the administrative burden without losing the liquidity advantage.

**Also raised:** private credit / first-mortgage lending inside the SMSF — ~9–10% versus 3.5–4% on cash or term deposit, first charge, on a portion of the portfolio only. Ross has raised this previously and uses it himself. Not a recommendation; flagged for consideration.

**Government co-contribution (Miriam):** income ~$15,000. A **$1,000 personal after-tax contribution attracts up to $500** from the government, subject to the income test (phases out between roughly $0 and $45,000). Effectively a 50% guaranteed return. Same strategy worth using with the children as a financial-education exercise — and as leverage for funding their insurance from super.
