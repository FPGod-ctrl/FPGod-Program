## 1. Purpose of this guide

A good estate plan makes sure the **right assets reach the right people, at the right time, with the least tax and conflict**. It is more than a Will — super, insurance, trusts, ownership structures and powers of attorney all interact. This guide outlines common strategies; it is general only, and the legal documents must be drafted by a solicitor.

---

## 2. An estate plan has moving parts

| Component | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Will** | Directs assets you own personally |
| **Superannuation** | Does **not** automatically pass under your Will — controlled by nominations |
| **Insurance** | Proceeds can go to a nominated beneficiary, the estate, or a policy owner |
| **Jointly owned assets** | Pass by survivorship, outside the Will |
| **Trusts / companies** | Controlled by succession of control, not the Will |
| **Powers of attorney** | Manage your affairs **while you are alive** but incapacitated |

## 3. Strategy 1 — Have a valid, current Will

**The strategy.** Put in place (and keep updated) a valid Will that clearly distributes your personal estate.

**Who it may suit.** Everyone — especially after marriage, divorce, children, or buying property.

**How it works.** A Will appoints an executor and directs personal assets. Without one, intestacy laws decide — often not as you'd wish.

**Things to consider.** Review after major life events; note that marriage/divorce can revoke or alter a Will; super and jointly held assets usually fall **outside** it.

*Drafted by a solicitor.*

---

## 4. Strategy 2 — Testamentary trusts

**The strategy.** Use a Will that creates a **testamentary trust** on death, rather than gifting assets outright.

**Who it may suit.** People with children (especially minors or grandchildren), blended families, or beneficiaries exposed to relationship/creditor risk.

**How it works.** Assets pass into a trust controlled by trustees you choose. Benefits: **asset protection** (e.g. on a beneficiary's divorce or bankruptcy) and **tax-effective income** — income distributed to minor beneficiaries from a testamentary trust is taxed at **adult marginal rates** (not penalty minor rates), which can be very tax-effective for grandchildren.

**Things to consider.** More complex and costly than a basic Will; benefits depend on correct drafting and trustee choice.

*Drafted by a solicitor.*

---

## 5. Strategy 3 — Enduring Power of Attorney & guardianship

**The strategy.** Appoint **enduring powers of attorney** (financial) and a medical/guardianship decision-maker.

**Who it may suit.** Everyone — critical if illness or injury affects capacity.

**How it works.** An enduring financial attorney can manage money/assets if you lose capacity; a medical/guardianship appointment covers health and lifestyle decisions. These operate **while you are alive**.

**Things to consider.** Choose trustworthy attorneys; documents are state-based; especially important where a health diagnosis exists.

*Drafted by a solicitor.*

---

## 6. Strategy 4 — Super death benefit nominations

**The strategy.** Put **valid binding death benefit nominations** in place so your super goes where you intend.

**Who it may suit.** Everyone with super.

**How it works.** Super **does not pass under your Will** unless directed to your estate. A **binding** nomination compels the trustee to pay as directed; a **non-lapsing** binding nomination doesn't expire after 3 years. You can nominate dependants directly, or your **legal personal representative** (estate) to flow through a testamentary trust.

**Things to consider.** Nominations must be valid and current; coordinate with the Will and the death-benefits-tax position (below).

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 & Regs (death benefit nominations).*

## 7. Strategy 5 — Minimise super death-benefits tax

**The strategy.** Reduce the tax adult, non-dependant children would pay on your super, in advance.

**Who it may suit.** People whose super will ultimately pass to adult children.

**How it works.** A death benefit to a **tax dependant** (e.g. spouse) is **tax-free**. To a **non-dependant**, the **taxable component** is taxed at **15% + 2% Medicare (17%)** if paid directly from the fund, or **15%** (no Medicare) if paid via the estate. Levers: nominate **spouse-first**; pay children's share **via the estate**; and use the **re-contribution strategy** (after 60, withdraw and re-contribute as a non-concessional contribution to lift the tax-free component — remembering the **proportioning rule** means a withdrawal is drawn proportionally from both components, not just the taxable one).

**Things to consider.** Re-contribution is subject to NCC caps, total-super-balance and age limits; benefits crystallise on death and depend on current law.

*Legislative basis: ITAA 1997 s302-145 (death benefits to non-dependants); s307-125 (proportioning).*

## 8. Strategy 6 — Reversionary pensions

**The strategy.** Make a pension **reversionary** to your spouse so income continues automatically on death.

**Who it may suit.** Couples drawing account-based pensions.

**How it works.** The pension continues to the surviving spouse (a tax dependant) — generally tax-free — with a 12-month window before it counts to their Transfer Balance Cap.

**Things to consider.** Coordinate with nominations and the estate plan; the survivor's own cap applies after the grace period.

*Legislative basis: ITAA 1997 Subdiv 294-B; SIS Regs.*

---

## 9. Strategy 7 — Review ownership structures & jointly held assets

**The strategy.** Understand how each asset passes — many do **not** go through your Will.

**Who it may suit.** Anyone with joint property, trusts, or company interests.

**How it works.** Joint tenancy assets pass by **survivorship**; trust/company assets pass by **succession of control** (e.g. who becomes appointor/director), not under the Will. Aligning these with your intentions avoids surprises.

**Things to consider.** Tenants-in-common vs joint tenants has very different outcomes; trust deeds govern succession of control.

*Drafted/reviewed by a solicitor.*

---

## 10. Strategy 8 — Coordinate insurance with the estate

**The strategy.** Make sure life insurance proceeds land where intended and tax-effectively.

**Who it may suit.** Anyone holding life/TPD cover, in or out of super.

**How it works.** Cover held **inside super** is paid as a death benefit subject to nomination and the death-benefits-tax rules; cover held **personally** can be paid to a nominated beneficiary or the estate. Structure ownership and beneficiaries to match the plan.

**Things to consider.** Insurance in super can be taxed to non-dependants; review beneficiary designations alongside the Will.

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993; ITAA 1997 (death benefits).*

---

## 11. Strategy 9 — Business succession & buy-sell agreements

**The strategy.** For business owners, put a **buy-sell (succession) agreement** in place, usually funded by insurance.

**Who it may suit.** Owners of a business with co-owners/partners.

**How it works.** A buy-sell agreement sets how an owner's share transfers on death/disability, with insurance providing the funds — giving the family value and remaining owners control.

**Things to consider.** Requires legal and tax structuring (CGT, ownership of policies); review as the business changes.

*Drafted by a solicitor/accountant.*

---

## 12. Strategy 10 — Blended families & equalisation

**The strategy.** Use trusts, life interests and beneficiary structuring to provide fairly across a blended family.

**Who it may suit.** Remarried couples, families with children from prior relationships.

**How it works.** Tools such as testamentary trusts, **life interests** (e.g. a partner can live in the home for life, with capital ultimately to children) and careful nominations balance competing needs and reduce the risk of disputes/claims.

**Things to consider.** Family-provision claims are a real risk — legal advice is essential.

*Drafted by a solicitor.*

---

## 13. Strategy 11 — Gifting, advance equalisation & records

**The strategy.** Consider lifetime gifting/loans (often via a trust) and keep clear records.

**Who it may suit.** People wanting to help children now or simplify the estate.

**How it works.** A **documented loan** (e.g. from a family/testamentary trust) can help a child into housing while protecting the capital on a relationship breakdown, unlike an outright gift. Keeping an up-to-date asset register and document list eases administration.

**Things to consider.** Gifting can affect Age Pension means testing; document loans properly; review beneficiaries and records regularly.

## 14. General risks & things to consider

- **It's not just the Will** — super, insurance, jointly owned and trust assets pass outside it; coordinate everything.
- **Legal drafting required** — Wills, trusts and POAs must be prepared by a solicitor.
- **Tax** — death-benefits tax and CGT can erode an estate without planning.
- **Currency** — review after every major life event and law change.
- **Personal advice** — obtain personal financial and legal advice tailored to you before acting.

---

## 15. Glossary

- **Testamentary trust** — a trust created by a Will, operating on death; provides asset protection and tax-effective income to beneficiaries.
- **Binding death benefit nomination** — a direction that compels the super trustee to pay your benefit as specified.
- **Tax dependant / non-dependant** — determines whether a super death benefit is tax-free or taxable.
- **Proportioning rule** — a super withdrawal is drawn from tax-free and taxable components in the same proportion as the whole interest.
- **Enduring power of attorney** — authority to manage your affairs if you lose capacity.
- **Reversionary pension** — a pension that continues automatically to a nominated beneficiary on death.
- **Life interest** — a right to use/benefit from an asset for life, with capital passing to others afterwards.
