## 3. The arrangement at a glance

The Fund borrows under a Limited Recourse Borrowing Arrangement (LRBA) to acquire a single commercial property, which is held by a custodian (holding trustee) on a bare trust for the Fund. The property is then leased to the members' own business at market rent. ## 4. Rule — SMSF with a corporate trustee  [SATISFIED]

The Fund must remain an SMSF (≤6 members; all members are directors of the corporate trustee, and all directors are members). A corporate trustee is appropriate and generally required for an SMSF that borrows and holds property — title and borrower are clearly held in the company's name, and membership changes are simpler. **Confirm** each member/director holds a **director ID** and has signed the **trustee declaration (s104A)**, and the Fund is ATO-registered and elected to be regulated.

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 s17A, s104A.*

## 5. Rule — the property must be, and remain, business real property  [MUST MAINTAIN]

An SMSF generally **cannot acquire assets from related parties** and is limited to **5% in-house assets** — but **business real property (BRP)** is the key exception: an SMSF may acquire BRP at **market value** and **lease it to a related party** (without it being an in-house asset), provided the property is used **wholly and exclusively in one or more businesses**.

**What must hold true:**
- The "Administrative Office" use must meet the **"wholly and exclusively in business"** test — **no private or residential use**, at any time.
- The acquisition must be at **market value** (independent valuation — see §13).

If the property ever ceases to be BRP (e.g. partial private use), the related-party acquisition/lease can **breach s66** and become an **in-house asset** — a serious compliance breach. BRP status must be confirmed at the start and **maintained for the life of the arrangement**.

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 s66 (related-party acquisitions; BRP exception s66(2)(b)); s66(5) (BRP definition); s71, Part 8 (in-house assets; BRP lease exception). ATO guidance: SMSFR 2009/1.*

## 6. Rule — the LRBA and the off-the-plan asset  [CONFIRM BEFORE DRAWDOWN]

A complying LRBA requires: borrowing applied to acquire a **single acquirable asset**; the asset held by a **custodian** on trust; the Fund holding the **beneficial interest** and the **right to acquire legal title** once the loan is repaid; the lender's recourse on default **limited to that asset**; and **no borrowed money used to improve or construct** the asset.

### Off-the-plan — fact-check against the law and your documents

**The law (SMSFR 2012/1).** The ATO **accepts** that an **off-the-plan strata unit or single-title block of land**, acquired under a **contract of sale** with a **deposit and the balance payable at settlement**, can be funded under **one** LRBA. **The trap:** if the purchase is instead structured as an **option** over off-the-plan property, the *option* is the single acquirable asset, and acquiring the property on exercise of the option is a **different asset** that would require a **separate LRBA**. So the contract structure matters.

**What your attached documents confirm (this part looks sound):**
- The **Single Acquirable Asset confirmation letter** describes the purchase as a **Contract of Sale** (with a Deed of Variation) for **Lot 29** — "one single parcel of real property (one lot)" — with a **separate title to issue before settlement**.
- The **Holding Trust Deed** (KLM Super Property Trust) records the **Asset** as 41 Watt Road, Mornington (C15) and requires the Beneficiary to ensure it is a **single acquirable asset**.
- The **LRBA Structure Confirmation letter** confirms the custodian/holding-trust structure and the intent to satisfy s67A.

On their face, these support a compliant **single-asset, off-the-plan, contract-of-sale** acquisition under one LRBA — consistent with SMSFR 2012/1.

**What the documents do NOT independently confirm — verify these before drawdown:**
1. **Contract, not an option.** Confirm the Contract of Sale is a **direct purchase** (deposit + balance at settlement), **not an option** (the letter implies a contract of sale — verify it is).
2. **Purchaser = the custodian.** Confirm the **holding trustee (KLM Super Property Pty Ltd)** is the **named purchaser** on the Contract of Sale (or that it is correctly the acquiring party), and that the bare trust was in place at the right time. *(The Contract of Sale was not provided.)*
3. **Same asset throughout.** Confirm the **Deed of Variation does not change the acquirable asset** (the letter says it "remains Lot 29" — confirm the variation only adjusts terms, not the identity of the asset).
4. **Borrowing funds settlement only.** Confirm **no borrowed money funds construction/progress payments or improvements** — borrowing is drawn at **settlement** to acquire the completed, titled lot.
5. **Duty & timing.** Confirm the **stamp-duty** treatment of the bare-trust/custodian arrangement (VIC) and that timing avoids double duty — **solicitor/SMSF specialist to confirm**.

**Net:** the off-the-plan single-LRBA approach is **acceptable to the ATO** for this kind of single-lot contract, and your documents support that it is a single acquirable asset. Obtain **written confirmation from the SMSF specialist/solicitor on points 1–5** before the loan is drawn. **[Critical.]**

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 s67A, s67B (LRBAs); Part 7 (borrowing). ATO guidance: SMSFR 2012/1.*

## 10. Rule — the Fund must remain an "Australian superannuation fund" (residency)  [MONITOR]

For the Fund to stay complying it must, at all times, be an **"Australian superannuation fund"**. This is a **3-part test (ITAA 1997 s295-95)** — **all three** must be met:

1. **Establishment / asset test.** The fund was established in Australia, **or** has at least one asset in Australia. *(Satisfied — the fund holds Australian property.)*
2. **Central management & control (CM&C) ordinarily in Australia.** The fund's **strategic, high-level decisions** (e.g. setting the investment strategy) must **ordinarily** be made in Australia. There is a **"safe harbour" (s295-95(4))** for a **temporary** absence of trustees of **up to 2 years** — but this is **not automatic**: facts matter, and a temporary absence over 2 years does not automatically fail (and under 2 years does not automatically pass).
3. **Active member test.** Either the fund has **no active members**, **or** at least **50%** of the fund (by market value of assets, or amounts that would be payable) is attributable to **active members who are Australian residents**. An **"active member"** is a member for whom contributions are being made (or who is contributing).

**Failure of any limb makes the Fund non-complying — with a tax hit of roughly 45% of the fund's value (and its income).** This is one of the most damaging breaches in super, so it must be monitored.

### How to determine and monitor residency (what you must do)
- **Confirm where each member ordinarily resides now**, and whether any member is likely to **move or spend extended time overseas**. *(The notes refer to contacting an "uncle in Thailand" — confirm whether any member is, or will be, overseas, and for how long.)* **[Critical — TO CONFIRM each member's residence.]**
- **Document that trustee/director decisions are made in Australia** (board minutes/resolutions signed in Australia).
- **Identify the active members** (those receiving contributions) and confirm **Australian-resident active members hold ≥50%** of the fund.
- **If a member goes (or plans to go) overseas**, act early — practical options include:
  - **Appoint an enduring power of attorney (EPOA)** to an Australian resident to act as trustee/director in the absent member's place (helps keep CM&C in Australia);
  - **Cease contributions** for the overseas member so they are **not an "active member"** (protects the active-member test); and/or
  - **Roll the member's benefit** to an APRA-regulated fund.
  - **Caution:** with most of the Fund tied up in **one illiquid, geared property**, rolling a member out is difficult — so **plan residency well ahead of any move.**
- **Watch the 2-year safe harbour** for any absence.

> **Note on proposed reform.** A measure was announced (2021/22 Budget) to **extend the CM&C safe harbour from 2 to 5 years and remove the active member test**. **As at late 2025 this is still not law** — so the **current 2-year safe harbour and active-member test apply** and must be relied on. We will update you if it is legislated.

## 11. Risks & consequences (summary)

- **Concentration / single-asset risk** — most of your retirement savings sit in one property in one location.
- **Gearing risk** — borrowing magnifies losses as well as gains; rising rates increase repayments.
- **Liquidity risk** — if rent + contributions don't cover repayments and costs, the Fund may be forced to sell.
- **Tenant / business risk** — your business is the tenant and you are the members; a business downturn hits the Fund's income and your retirement at the same time.
- **Market-rent / arm's-length risk** — the lease must stay at market rent, paid in full and on time.
- **BRP risk** — the property must remain wholly and exclusively used in business.
- **Off-the-plan / single-asset risk** — confirm the §6 points before drawdown.
- **Residency risk** — see §10; a member overseas can make the Fund non-complying.
- **Legislative & cost risk** — super/LRBA/tax rules can change; SMSF property + LRBA carry meaningful ongoing costs.

## 19. Appendix C — glossary

- **SMSF** — self-managed superannuation fund (≤6 members, all trustees/directors).
- **LRBA** — limited recourse borrowing arrangement: the Fund borrows to buy a single asset held in a holding trust; the lender's recourse is limited to that asset.
- **Holding (bare) trust / custodian** — holds legal title to the asset for the Fund until the loan is repaid.
- **Business real property (BRP)** — land/buildings used wholly and exclusively in one or more businesses; can be acquired from, and leased to, a related party.
- **In-house asset** — a loan to/investment in/lease with a related party; limited to 5% of fund assets (BRP leases are excepted).
- **Single acquirable asset** — the one asset an LRBA may fund; for off-the-plan, the completed lot under a contract of sale (not an option).
- **Arm's length** — genuine commercial terms (here, market rent paid in full and on time).
- **Australian superannuation fund** — the residency status the Fund must keep (establishment/asset, CM&C in Australia, active-member tests).
- **Sole purpose test** — the Fund must be maintained solely to provide retirement (and death) benefits.
