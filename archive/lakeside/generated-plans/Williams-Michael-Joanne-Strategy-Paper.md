## 7. Strategy 1 — Enduring Powers of Attorney & incapacity planning [URGENT]

**Why this is the #1 action.** The Client Profile records **no Powers of Attorney**. Given Michael's health, this is the most urgent item in the plan:

- If Michael lost capacity without an **enduring power of attorney (financial)**, Joanne could not simply step in — she would face a **VCAT administration application**, with delay and cost at the worst possible time.
- An SMSF member who loses capacity must generally have their **legal personal representative (attorney) appointed as trustee/director** to keep the fund compliant. Without an EPOA, the **SMSF's compliance is at risk**.
- A **medical treatment decision-maker / advance care directive** ensures Joanne (or a nominated person) can make health decisions.

**Execution.** Doug Patrick (already engaged for the Wills) should prepare **enduring financial POAs and medical decision-maker appointments for both Michael and Joanne** — these are typically bundled with the Wills at modest additional cost and **should not wait** for the Wills to be finalised.

## 8. Strategy 2 — Binding death benefit nominations on the SMSF [URGENT]

**Mechanics.** Superannuation does **not** pass under a Will. On a member's death the SMSF trustee decides who receives the benefit — **unless** a valid **binding death benefit nomination (BDBN)** directs it. For this household, the recommended pattern is:

- **First death:** benefit to the **surviving spouse** — paid **tax-free** as a tax dependant, and keeps capital in the survivor's pension.
- **On the second death (or where appropriate):** benefit to the **legal personal representative (the estate)**, so it flows into the **testamentary trust** for Luke and Brittany — rather than being paid **directly** to them (which would be taxed at up to **17%** on the taxable component **and** bypass the trust's asset protection).

**Eligibility / execution checklist.**
- Confirm the **SMSF trust deed** permits binding nominations (and whether lapsing or non-lapsing). **[TO CONFIRM]**
- Prepare BDBNs for **both members**, witnessed correctly.
- Coordinate the nominations with the **testamentary trust Wills** so the routing is consistent.
- Pair with the **re-contribution strategy** (Section 10) to shrink the taxable component before benefits ever reach the children.

**Legislative basis.** SIS Act 1993 / SIS Regs (death benefit payment rules); ITAA 1997 s302-145 (death benefits to non-dependants — taxable component taxed at 15% + Medicare).

## 10. Strategy 4 — Commence account-based pensions (the central engine)

**Mechanics.** Accumulation-phase SMSF earnings are taxed at 15% (10% on discounted capital gains). Once a member commences an **account-based pension (ABP)** within their $2.0M Transfer Balance Cap, earnings on those assets become **tax-free (0%)**, and pension payments to a 65+ member are tax-free in their hands.

**Indicative benefit.**
| Phase | Annual tax on ~$47,000 of fund earnings (7.2% on $649,600) | Saving |
|---|---|---|
| 100% accumulation | ~$7,000 (15%) | baseline |
| 100% pension phase | **$0** | **~$7,000 p.a.** |

**Minimum drawdown (ages 65–74 = 5%).**
| Member | Pension balance | Min. annual drawdown |
|---|---|---|
| Michael | $576,703 | ~$28,800 |
| Joanne | $72,897 | ~$3,600 |
| Combined | $649,600 | ~$32,500 |

**Recommended sequencing.** Commence ABPs for **both** members now (Joanne included — the ~$3,600 minimum is trivial). After the home sale, make the **downsizer into accumulation** and commence a **second pension** with that capital — this lets the lump-sum inflows land first, then convert cleanly to pension phase.

**Legislative basis.** ITAA 1997 s295-385 (exempt current pension income); ITAA 1997 Subdiv 294-B (Transfer Balance Cap); SIS Regs (minimum drawdown percentages).

## 12. Strategy 6 — Re-contribution: lifting the tax-free component for the children

**Why it matters.** Super death benefits paid to **non-dependants** (Luke and Brittany) are taxed on the **taxable component** at up to **17%** (15% + Medicare); the **tax-free component passes entirely tax-free**. A re-contribution withdraws a lump sum (tax-free for a 65+ member) and re-contributes it as an NCC, converting **taxable → tax-free** component permanently.

**Indicative impact (illustrative — assumes Michael's $576,703 is largely taxable component [TO CONFIRM proportions from SMSF member records]).**
| | Amount | Death-benefits tax to children @17% |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable component today | ~$576,700 | ~$98,000 |
| After a $360,000 re-contribution (3-yr NCC bring-forward) | Taxable ~$216,700 / Tax-free ~$360,000 | ~$36,800 — **saving ~$61,000** |

**Mechanics & limits.**
- Michael (67) is **under 75**, so NCCs including a $360,000 bring-forward are available, subject to his TSB at the relevant test date.
- Best executed **after Michael retires**, and can be paired with the downsizer year — the **downsizer does not consume NCC cap**, so a downsizer and re-contribution can both run in the same period.
- Document the withdrawal and re-contribution trail for clean component tracking.
- Consider a **partial re-contribution to Joanne** as well (balance equalisation — Section 14), provided her TBC capacity is preserved.

**Legislative basis.** ITAA 1997 s307-125 (proportioning rule); ITAA 1997 s292 (NCC cap & bring-forward).

## 13. Strategy 7 — SMSF structure decision (retain vs wind up)

The email chain records Michael's understanding that *"our JEMJWILLIAMS SMSF will cease to exist"* and *"Lakeside turns our super into a family trust and manages the investments."* This is the point in the file most needing careful, **conflict-aware** advice:

- **Super stays in super.** Whether in an SMSF or a managed/retail/industry pension, the money stays in the superannuation system — it does **not** become a "family trust." The *testamentary trust* is an **estate** structure that only operates on death; it is separate from where super is invested during life.
- **The real decision** is **retain the SMSF** (low ongoing cost — FY25 accountancy/audit ~$3,616; full control; ~7.2% history) **or wind up** and move to a Lakeside-managed pension (simpler administration, professional management, but ongoing advice/management fees and loss of direct control).

**Adviser framing (recommended for the client conversation):** present both paths neutrally, **show the fee differences explicitly**, and be transparent that a Lakeside-managed solution generates ongoing revenue for the firm. The **investment de-risking** (Section 14) can be achieved **inside the existing SMSF** — so a wind-up should be justified on administration/simplicity grounds, **not** used as a backdoor to a re-weighting the SMSF could do itself.

> **Restructure timing —** If a wind-up is chosen, do it **after** pension phase commences. Selling the cash-heavy portfolio then occurs at **0% CGT** (pension-phase earnings are exempt) — the cleanest possible time to restructure. Doing it in accumulation would expose gains to 10–15% tax unnecessarily.

## 14. Strategy 8 — Investment structure review: de-risk the SMSF & equalise balances

### Asset allocation
The fund is **~60–65% cash** ($410,214 in ANZ term deposits and savers) plus a concentrated equity sleeve dominated by **CBA (~$90,000, overweight)**. Two issues:
1. **Too much cash for a 25–30 year horizon** — a 60%+ cash weighting won't keep pace with inflation; the household's 7.2% history was achieved largely on the equity sleeve **despite** the cash drag.
2. **Single-stock concentration** — a ~$90k CBA position is a large idiosyncratic bet for a retirement fund.

**Recommended — a "bucket" strategy for pension phase:**
- **Bucket 1 (1–2 yrs of pension payments):** cash / at-call (from the existing large cash holding).
- **Bucket 2 (3–7 yrs):** defensive / income (term deposits, fixed interest, conservative funds).
- **Bucket 3 (7+ yrs):** diversified growth (broad equities / managed funds) — funded partly by **trimming the CBA overweight**.

Re-weighting is best executed **in pension phase** (0% CGT on required sales), and can be done **inside the SMSF** regardless of the Section 13 structure decision.

### Balance equalisation
Joanne's balance (~$73k) is far smaller than Michael's (~$577k). Building Joanne's balance (via spouse contribution, spouse split, downsizer to Joanne, partial re-contribution to Joanne) **uses both members' $2.0M Transfer Balance Caps** and improves flexibility and resilience (e.g. if the first death is Michael's, more of the couple's wealth is already in Joanne's name and pension).
