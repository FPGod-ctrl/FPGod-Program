## 8. Strategy 2 — Binding death benefit nominations on the SMSF  [IMMEDIATE]

**Recommendation.** Each of you make a **valid binding death benefit nomination (BDBN)** on the SMSF directing your benefit **first to your surviving spouse**, and **on the second death (or where appropriate) to your legal personal representative (estate)** so it passes through the testamentary trust to Luke and Brittany.

**Why this is appropriate for you.** Superannuation does **not** pass under a Will. Without a binding nomination the SMSF trustee decides who receives the benefit:
- **Spouse first** — paid **tax-free** (a spouse is a tax dependant) and keeps capital in the survivor's pension.
- **To the estate on second death** — so the benefit flows into the **testamentary trust** (asset protection + tax-effective income to grandchildren) rather than being paid **directly** to the children, which would be taxed at up to **17%** on the taxable component **and** bypass the trust.
This pairs with the re-contribution strategy (Section 12), which shrinks the taxable component before benefits reach the children.

**How to implement.** Confirm the **SMSF trust deed** permits binding (and ideally non-lapsing) nominations **[TO CONFIRM]**; prepare and witness BDBNs for both members; align them with the testamentary trust Wills.

**Risks / consequences.** A BDBN must be valid and current to bind the trustee; lapsing nominations must be renewed. Paying to the estate exposes the benefit to any estate creditors/challenges — managed by the testamentary trust structure.

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 & Regs; ITAA 1997 s302-145.*

**Recommendation.** On settlement of the Menzies Creek sale, each make a **downsizer contribution of up to $300,000 ($600,000 combined)** into super within 90 days.

| Test | Status |
|---|---|

| Within 90 days of settlement; election lodged at/before contribution | ✔ |
| Does **not** count toward the NCC cap; no TSB test; no work test | ✔ |

**Why this is appropriate for you.** It is the most effective way to move a large amount of capital into the low-tax super environment, and it sits **outside** the normal caps and balance limits. A $600,000 downsizer roughly **doubles** your super (to ~$1.25M), lifting tax-free retirement income to ~**$62,500 p.a.** at a 5% drawdown — which, with Joanne's income and your cash buffer, comfortably meets the **$75,000** goal.

> **⚠ Capital gains tax on the ~10-acre land — and how to minimise it.** The main-residence (CGT) exemption is **capped at 2 hectares (≈ 4.9 acres), including the land under the house** — **ITAA 1997 s118-120**. This is a **flat national rule: it does not change for metropolitan vs regional, postcode or zoning** — a 10-acre block at Menzies Creek gets the same 2-hectare cap as one in metro Melbourne. Your block is **~10 acres (~4.05 ha)**, so roughly **half the land sits outside the cap** and the gain on that part is taxable.
>
> **Crucially, the excess can be apportioned by *value*, not just area (ATO TD 1999/67).** You choose which 2 hectares is exempt (it must include the house), and where the parts can be separately valued the gain is split **on the basis of the valuation**. Because the **house block is worth far more per acre than the bare back paddock**, a value-based apportionment means the **taxable portion is likely well below 50%** — so the real CGT is probably **materially lower** than a crude area split suggests.
>
> *Illustrative only (1995 purchase, ~$250,000 cost base — all [TO CONFIRM by valuer/accountant]):*
> | Apportionment basis | Taxable share of the ~$950k gain | After 50% discount | Split 50/50 | Indicative tax (low-income year) |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Area (~50%) — the crude upper estimate | ~$475,000 | ~$237,500 | ~$118,750 each | **~$57,600 combined** |
> | **Value-based (e.g. bare excess land ~20% of value)** | **~$190,000** | **~$95,000** | **~$47,500 each** | **~$22,000–$25,000 combined** |
>
> **So a proper valuation could roughly halve (or better) the CGT.** Two further levers reduce it: **(a) sell in a low-income retirement year** (the gain is taxed at your marginal rate — near-nil other income = low tax), and **(b)** the value-based apportionment above. **Downsizer eligibility is *not* affected** by a partial CGT liability (the rules only require the home to have qualified for the exemption in part). **Confirm no part of the land was ever income-producing** (farming/agistment/home business with a dedicated area), as that can reduce the private-use exemption. **[TO CONFIRM land area, purchase date, cost base, and obtain a valuer's value-based apportionment — model with Cooper & Lockhart.]**

**Net-cash sequencing.** Downsizer can only come from sale proceeds, which must also buy your next home — so the practical contribution depends on the replacement price (see the property options in Section 24).

**How to implement.** **Before listing**, obtain the original cost base and a **valuer's value-based apportionment** of the land (s118-120 / TD 1999/67) and have Cooper & Lockhart model the CGT; **time the sale for a low-income retirement year** to minimise it; then lodge the downsizer election within 90 days of settlement and commence a second pension with the contributed capital.

**Risks / consequences.** The contributed amount is **preserved in super**; part of the land gain may be taxable; the amount available depends on the replacement home.

*Legislative basis: ITAA 1997 s292-102 (downsizer); s118-110 (main residence exemption); s118-120 (adjacent-land 2-hectare cap); ATO TD 1999/67 (value-based apportionment of land over 2 ha); Div 115 (50% CGT discount).*

## 37. Appendix E — glossary

- **Account-based pension (ABP)** — a retirement-phase income stream from super; earnings tax-free within the Transfer Balance Cap.
- **Accumulation phase** — the pre-pension phase; fund earnings taxed at up to 15%.
- **Binding death benefit nomination (BDBN)** — a valid direction binding the super trustee on who receives your death benefit.
- **Concessional contribution** — a before-tax contribution (employer, salary sacrifice, or personal deductible); taxed 15% in the fund.
- **Non-concessional contribution (NCC)** — an after-tax contribution; not taxed in the fund.
- **Downsizer contribution** — a one-off contribution from the sale of a long-held main residence; outside the caps.
- **Transfer Balance Cap (TBC)** — the lifetime limit ($2.0M) on what can be moved into tax-free retirement phase.
- **Taxable / tax-free component** — the parts of a super balance; the taxable part can be taxed to non-dependant beneficiaries on death.
- **Re-contribution strategy** — withdrawing and re-contributing to convert taxable to tax-free component.
- **Testamentary trust** — a trust created by a Will, operating on death; provides asset protection and tax-effective income.
- **Enduring Power of Attorney** — authority for someone to manage your affairs if you lose capacity.
