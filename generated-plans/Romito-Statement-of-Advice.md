## 11. Strategy 5 — Investment-property CGT plan  [MEDIUM]

**Recommendation.** If you sell an investment property to fund retirement, **sell it in a year when your taxable income is low (i.e., once retired)** — and **contribute the proceeds to super** (as non-concessional and/or deductible contributions). Decide deliberately between **selling for retirement income** and **retaining the properties for the children**.

**Why this is appropriate for you.** Investment properties do **not** get the main-residence exemption, so a gain is taxable. But:
- Held >12 months, you get the **50% CGT discount**, and the gain is **split 50/50** between you (joint owners).
- The gain is added to your **marginal income** in the year of sale — so selling while earning ~$155k/$115k could push it into the **top brackets**, whereas selling **after you retire (near-zero income)** is taxed far more lightly.
- *Illustrative (Reservoir, assuming ~$150k cost base — [TO CONFIRM]):* sale ~$650k − cost ~$150k = ~$500k gain; less 50% discount = ~$250k assessable; split = ~$125k each — taxed at **near-top rates if working**, but at **much lower average rates in a zero-income retirement year**.

## 12. Strategy 6 — Transition to retirement / pension phase at 60  [AT AGE 60]

**Recommendation.** From **age 60** (your preservation age), commence a **transition-to-retirement (TTR) income stream**, and on full retirement convert to **account-based pensions**.

**Why this is appropriate for you.** In **pension phase, fund earnings and the income you draw are tax-free**. A TTR from 60 can let you **reduce work hours while topping up income**, and (combined with continued concessional contributions) is tax-efficient in the lead-up to retirement. Your balances are far below the **$2.0M Transfer Balance Cap** each, so the full amounts can move to tax-free phase.

**How to implement.** Commence TTR at 60 (minimum 4% / maximum 10% drawdown); convert to full account-based pensions on retirement; sequence with the property sale and contributions so the largest balance is in tax-free phase.

**Risks / consequences.** TTR rules and caps can change; pension capital remains market-exposed; minimum drawdowns apply once in pension phase.

*Legislative basis: ITAA 1997 s295-385 (ECPI); Subdiv 294-B (TBC); SIS Regs (TTR).*

## 18. Risks, consequences and things to consider

- **Investment risk** — super remains market-exposed; de-risking reduces but does not remove volatility; returns are not guaranteed.
- **Sequencing risk** — a market fall just before retirement is the key risk an 80%-growth portfolio carries; de-risking addresses it.
- **Legislative risk** — super, contribution caps, tax and estate rules can change.
- **Capital gains tax** — selling a property triggers CGT; timing (working vs retired) materially changes the bill.
- **Insurance** — John's cover is irreplaceable; a careless rollover could cancel it; pre-existing-condition clauses may limit claims.
- **Concentration & liquidity** — much of your wealth is in property (illiquid); keeping both properties for the kids reduces retirement income flexibility.
- **Health & timing** — estate documents, nominations and de-risking are time-sensitive given John's diagnosis.
- **Fees** — the managed-super option costs more than your current funds; weigh the advice value against the cost.
- **If you do nothing** — John's super stays over-exposed to growth near retirement, the carry-forward tax window narrows and closes, your estate remains exposed, and your $100k income goal is less certain.

## 29. Appendix E — glossary

- **Carry-forward (catch-up) concessional contributions** — using unused concessional cap from the prior 5 years; available only if your total super balance is under $500,000 at the prior 30 June.
- **Concessional contribution** — a before-tax contribution (employer, salary sacrifice, personal deductible); taxed 15% in the fund.
- **Non-concessional contribution (NCC)** — an after-tax contribution; not taxed in the fund.
- **Transition to retirement (TTR)** — an income stream you can start at preservation age (60) while still working.
- **Account-based pension** — a retirement-phase income stream; earnings tax-free within the Transfer Balance Cap.
- **Transfer Balance Cap (TBC)** — the lifetime limit ($2.0M) on what can move into tax-free retirement phase.
- **Taxable / tax-free component** — parts of a super balance; the taxable part can be taxed to non-dependant beneficiaries on death.
- **CGT 50% discount** — halves the assessable capital gain on assets held >12 months.
- **Testamentary trust** — a trust created by a Will, operating on death; provides asset protection and tax-effective income to beneficiaries.
