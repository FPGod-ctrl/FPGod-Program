**Subject:** Clifford — your super has no beneficiary recorded, and a few other things worth checking

Hi Clifford,

My name is Tristan Biro, a financial adviser with Lakeside Financial. We haven't spoken before, so I'll get straight to the point.

Your FirstChoice Wholesale Personal Super account sits under our advisory, though **no ongoing advice fee is being charged on it**. So you haven't been paying for advice you weren't receiving — but it also means nobody has reviewed the account in some time. Having read your latest statement, there are four things I'd want to raise, and the first is the reason I'm writing today.

**1. You have no beneficiary nomination on the account.** Your statement reads, in plain terms, that no beneficiary details have been provided.

This matters more than most people realise. Superannuation does **not** pass under your Will — it's dealt with separately by the fund. Where no nomination exists, the trustee decides who receives it, based on their own assessment of your circumstances. That process takes time, the outcome may not be what you'd have chosen, and your family has very little say in it.

Your balance is **$88,013**. Putting a nomination in place takes about five minutes and costs nothing. Of everything on your statement, this is the one I'd fix first.

**2. A fifth of your super is in a geared share fund.** Around **20%** of your account sits in Acadian Geared Australian Core Equity — a fund that borrows to increase its exposure to shares. Gearing magnifies gains in a good year and losses in a bad one. It's a legitimate option, but it's the most aggressive thing on the FirstChoice menu, and at 65 it's worth confirming that's a position you've actively chosen.

More broadly, your account is **100% growth assets** — roughly half Australian shares, 40% global shares and 10% property, with no defensive allocation at all.

**3. You're holding eleven separate investment options, and the result was 3.74%.** That's a lot of moving parts for an account of this size, and it doesn't necessarily buy more diversification — several of those funds will hold much the same underlying shares at different fee levels. Your costs were **$972** for the year. The same exposure could very likely be held far more simply.

**4. Your account is still in accumulation phase.** At 65 you're past the age where super is locked away. In accumulation, earnings inside the fund are taxed at 15%; in pension phase they're generally tax-free. Whether moving makes sense depends on whether you want to draw an income — pension phase requires a minimum withdrawal each year — but it's a decision worth making deliberately rather than by default.

One smaller thing: you made a **$4,000 personal after-tax contribution** last year, following several years of none. Depending on your income at the time, claiming that as a deduction may have been more effective. Worth a look if you plan to contribute again.
