Your FirstChoice super account sits under our advisory, though **no ongoing advice fee is being charged on it**. So you haven't been paying for advice you weren't receiving — but it also means nobody has reviewed the account in some time. Three things stood out.

**1. You have no beneficiary nomination on the account.** Your statement says plainly that no beneficiary details have been provided.

Superannuation does **not** pass under your Will — the fund deals with it separately. Where no nomination exists, the trustee decides who receives it based on their own assessment of your circumstances. That takes time, the outcome may not be what you'd have chosen, and your family has little say in it.

Your balance is **$75,247**. Putting a nomination in place takes about five minutes and costs nothing. Of everything on your statement, this is what I'd fix first.

**2. You have a lot of unused contribution room.** Your contributions last year were around **$4,300**, all from your employer, against an annual cap of **$30,000**. That leaves a substantial amount unused.

Because your balance is under $500,000, you can generally also draw on unused room from the previous five years — and on your figures that's a considerable sum. Contributions made this way are tax deductible, so it's effectively a way of reducing your tax while building your super. The catch is that the oldest year's unused room drops off permanently each 30 June, so it's worth acting on rather than leaving.

**3. The account itself is in good shape.** A return of around **9.3%** for the year, held in a single option, costing **$389**. Nothing there I'd be rushing to change.

At 64 you're also at the point where super becomes accessible, which opens up some useful options worth understanding.
