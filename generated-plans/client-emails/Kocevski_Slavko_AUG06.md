**1. You have no beneficiary nomination on the account.** Your statement says, in plain terms, that no beneficiary details have been provided.

This is worth understanding. Superannuation does **not** pass under your Will — the fund deals with it separately. Where there's no nomination, the trustee decides who receives it based on their own assessment of your circumstances. That takes time, the outcome may not be what you'd have chosen, and your family has little say in it.

Your balance is **$52,540**. Putting a nomination in place takes about five minutes and costs nothing. Of everything on your statement, this is what I'd fix first.

Eight investment options for an account this size.** That's more moving parts than most people need, and it doesn't necessarily buy more diversification — several funds often hold much the same underlying shares at different fee levels. Your return was **4.87%** and costs were **$281** for the year. The same exposure could likely be held more simply.

Worth a look, as the oldest year's unused room drops off permanently each 30 June.

At 64 you're also at the point where super becomes accessible, which opens up some useful options worth understanding before you need them.
