Your FirstChoice Wholesale Personal Super account sits under our advisory, though **no ongoing advice fee is being charged on it**. So you haven't been paying for advice you weren't receiving — but it also means nobody has reviewed the account in some time. Three things stood out on your statement.

**1. Your concessional contributions are well above the annual cap.** Your statement shows roughly **$51,700** in 2024-25 and **$54,000** in 2025-26. The standard concessional cap is **$30,000** a year.

There's a perfectly good explanation for this if you've been using **carry-forward contributions** — unused cap from earlier years, which you're entitled to do while your total super balance is under $500,000. Your statement does show several earlier years with nothing contributed, so that would fit.

But if the carry-forward room has run out, contributions above the cap attract additional tax, and it isn't always obvious until an assessment arrives. Given the amounts involved, this is worth confirming properly — it's the main reason I'm writing.

**2. Thirteen investment options, and a 2.52% return.** That's a lot of moving parts for an account of this size, and the result was modest — several of those funds will hold much the same underlying shares at different fee levels. Your costs were **$532** for the year. The same exposure could almost certainly be held more simply, and more cheaply.

**3. At 63, you're close to some useful options.** You're past the age where super becomes accessible, which opens up choices around drawing an income while continuing to work, and around when the account moves out of accumulation phase. Worth understanding what's available before you need it rather than after.
