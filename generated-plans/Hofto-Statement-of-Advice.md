## 7. Strategy 1 — Enduring Powers of Attorney  [IMMEDIATE]

**Recommendation.** Put in place, for **both** of you, an **enduring Power of Attorney (financial)** and the relevant **medical/personal decision-maker** appointment.

**Why this is appropriate for you.** You have **none**, you run an **SMSF with a corporate trustee**, and you have both had **health events** (Peter's hip; Veronica's illness). If either of you lost capacity without an EPOA, the other could not simply step in — it would require a guardianship/administration application, with delay and cost. Critically, an SMSF member who loses capacity must have their **attorney appointed** as their replacement director to keep the fund **compliant**. Without an EPOA, your **SMSF's compliance is at risk**.

**How to implement.** Have a solicitor prepare enduring POAs for both of you, bundled with the Will update (Section 9). We will obtain copies and ensure the SMSF documentation references the attorneys.

**Risks / consequences.** Modest cost; effectiveness depends on correct drafting and your choice of attorney — the protection far outweighs it.

*Legislative basis: relevant State powers-of-attorney legislation; SIS Act 1993 s17A.*

## 8. Strategy 2 — Binding death benefit nominations on the SMSF  [IMMEDIATE]

**Recommendation.** Each of you make a **valid binding death benefit nomination (BDBN)** — **first to your surviving spouse** (tax-free), and **then to your estate** so that whatever remains passes under your Wills to your **nieces and nephews**.

**Why this is appropriate for you.** Superannuation does **not** pass under your Will, and your member statements record **no nomination**. In a two-member SMSF, on the first death the survivor becomes sole director with full discretion. A binding nomination:
- pays the survivor **tax-free** and keeps capital in their pension; and
- on the second death, directs the residual cleanly to your **estate** (and on to your nieces/nephews) rather than leaving it to be sorted out — which, as we discussed, can otherwise be *"a nightmare to get the monies."*

**How to implement.** Confirm the **SMSF deed** permits binding (ideally non-lapsing) nominations **[TO CONFIRM]**; prepare and witness BDBNs for both members; align them with the updated Wills.

**Risks / consequences.** A BDBN must be valid and current to bind the trustee; lapsing nominations need renewal.

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 & SIS Regs; ITAA 1997 s302-195 (death-benefit dependants).*

## 12. Strategy 6 — Transition-to-retirement (TTR) pension for Peter  [MEDIUM]

**Recommendation.** Peter commences a **transition-to-retirement (TTR) income stream** from his ~$700,000 balance — used to (a) support the loan-offset play (Section 17) and (b) begin the shift toward pension phase.

**Why this is appropriate for you.** Peter is **past preservation age (60)** but **still working**, so a TTR is the pension available to him. Pension payments are **tax-free** in his hands (over 60). The TTR lets him **access super now** (e.g. to park against the loan, Section 17) and, importantly, **have a pension running** ready to convert to a full account-based pension the moment he retires (Section 16).

The TTR's value here is **access and cash-flow**, not an earnings-tax saving.

## 14. Strategy 8 — Increase the commercial rent & free up company cash  [HIGH]
