## 1. Purpose of this guide

Insurance protects the engine behind every financial plan — **your ability to earn, and your family's security if something goes wrong**. This guide explains the main cover types, how to size and structure them, and the traps to avoid. It is general only; an actual recommendation requires a personal needs analysis.

---

## 2. The four main personal covers

| Cover | Pays out when… | Typically protects |
|---|---|---|
| **Life (death) cover** | You die (or are terminally ill) | Family, debts, dependants |
| **TPD** | You're totally & permanently disabled | Lump-sum needs if you can't work again |
| **Income protection (IP)** | Illness/injury stops you working | An ongoing % of your income |
| **Trauma / critical illness** | You suffer a defined serious condition | Lump sum for treatment/recovery |

---

## 3. Strategy 1 — Start with a needs analysis

**The strategy.** Size cover from **need**, not a default — calculate the gap between what your family would require and the resources already available.

**Who it may suit.** Everyone, especially those with dependants, debt or a sole income.

**How it works.** A common framework is **debts + income replacement + future costs (education, etc.) − existing assets/cover**. The result sets the sum insured for each cover type.

**Things to consider.** Needs change with life stage; over-insuring wastes premiums, under-insuring leaves a gap; review regularly.

---

## 4. Strategy 2 — Life (death) cover

**The strategy.** Hold enough life cover to clear debts and support dependants if you die.

**Who it may suit.** People with a mortgage, dependants or a single household income.

**How it works.** Pays a lump sum on death (or terminal illness). Can be held inside super (cash-flow friendly) or personally (more control, no preservation rules).

**Things to consider.** Need usually **falls over time** as debts reduce and assets grow; coordinate the beneficiary with your estate plan.

---

## 5. Strategy 3 — Total & Permanent Disability (TPD)

**The strategy.** Cover the lump-sum cost of never being able to work again.

**Who it may suit.** Income earners, particularly with debt or dependants.

**How it works.** Pays a lump sum if you're totally and permanently disabled under the policy definition. Definitions matter: **"own occupation"** is broader (and usually can't be held in super) vs **"any occupation."**

**Things to consider.** Check the definition; TPD inside super may have a more restrictive definition and tax on payout in some cases.

---

## 6. Strategy 4 — Income protection (IP)

**The strategy.** Replace a portion of your income (commonly up to ~70%) if illness or injury stops you working.

**Who it may suit.** Anyone who relies on their income — often the **most important** cover for working-age people.

**How it works.** Pays a monthly benefit after a **waiting period**, for a **benefit period** (e.g. 2 years, or to age 65). Premiums for IP are generally **tax-deductible** when held personally.

**Things to consider.** Longer waiting periods and shorter benefit periods cut premiums but increase your exposure; newer "income-protection" contracts differ from older "agreed value" ones — check terms.

*Tax note: personally held IP premiums are generally deductible; benefits are assessable income.*

---

## 7. Strategy 5 — Trauma / critical illness

**The strategy.** Provide a lump sum on diagnosis of a serious condition (e.g. cancer, heart attack, stroke).

**Who it may suit.** People who want a buffer for treatment, recovery time or reducing debt during illness.

**How it works.** Pays on diagnosis of a defined condition, regardless of whether you can work. Generally **cannot** be held inside super.

**Things to consider.** Definitions and condition lists vary widely between insurers; premiums are not tax-deductible.

---

## 8. Strategy 6 — Inside super vs outside super

**The strategy.** Choose where to hold each cover — inside superannuation, outside, or a mix.

**Who it may suit.** Everyone weighing cash flow against features and tax.

**How it works.** **Inside super:** premiums funded from your balance (easier cash flow), but reduces retirement savings, definitions can be narrower, and death cover paid to a non-dependant is taxed. **Outside super:** more comprehensive definitions and control, IP premiums deductible, but premiums paid from cash flow.

**Things to consider.** A common structure is **IP and some life/TPD in super** for cash flow, with **trauma and "own-occupation" TPD outside**. Beware default super cover lapsing on inactivity.

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 (insurance in super); ITAA 1997 (deductibility & death-benefit tax).*

---

## 9. Strategy 7 — Structure ownership correctly

**The strategy.** Get the **ownership and beneficiary** of each policy right for tax and estate outcomes.

**Who it may suit.** Couples, business owners, anyone coordinating insurance with their estate.

**How it works.** Options include self-ownership, super-fund ownership, cross-ownership (spouse owns the other's policy) and business structures. Ownership affects who receives proceeds, the tax treatment and whether proceeds form part of the estate.

**Things to consider.** TPD/trauma proceeds can have CGT implications depending on owner; align with the estate plan and any buy-sell agreement.

---

## 10. Strategy 8 — Stepped vs level premiums

**The strategy.** Choose a premium structure suited to how long you'll hold the cover.

**Who it may suit.** Anyone taking out or reviewing cover.

**How it works.** **Stepped** premiums start lower but rise with age; **level** premiums start higher but rise more slowly — often cheaper over the long term if cover is kept for many years.

**Things to consider.** Level isn't "fixed" (it still changes with repricing); the breakeven depends on how long you keep the policy.

---

## 11. Strategy 9 — Underwriting & pre-existing conditions

**The strategy.** Understand underwriting before changing anything — and **never cancel before replacement is in force**.

**Who it may suit.** Anyone reviewing, switching, or with a health change.

**How it works.** New cover is underwritten on your health; a new condition can mean exclusions, loadings or a decline. Existing in-force cover may therefore be **irreplaceable**.

**Things to consider — critical.** **Do not cancel or roll over existing cover until any replacement is confirmed accepted and in force.** Disclose accurately; check pre-existing-condition exclusions in default/super cover.

---

## 12. Strategy 10 — Review on life events

**The strategy.** Re-check cover whenever life changes, not just at renewal.

**Who it may suit.** Everyone.

**How it works.** Marriage, children, a mortgage, a business, divorce, or rising assets all change the need — usually less life/TPD over time, but IP remains important while you're working.

**Things to consider.** Avoid duplicate cover across multiple super funds; cancel only once replacement (if needed) is confirmed.

---

## 13. Strategy 11 — Beneficiaries & claims readiness

**The strategy.** Nominate beneficiaries correctly and keep policy details accessible for a smooth claim.

**Who it may suit.** All policyholders.

**How it works.** For super-held cover, a valid (preferably binding) nomination directs the death benefit; for personally held policies, the owner/beneficiary controls payment. Keep schedules, definitions and contacts on file.

**Things to consider.** Review nominations alongside the Will; understand each policy's definitions and waiting/benefit periods before you need to claim.

*Legislative basis: SIS Act 1993 & Regs (nominations).*

---

## 14. General risks & things to consider

- **Underinsurance** — most common gap is income protection and adequate life/TPD for debts and dependants.
- **Don't cancel early** — existing cover can be irreplaceable after a health change; confirm replacement in force first.
- **Definitions matter** — "own" vs "any" occupation, condition lists, waiting/benefit periods drive whether a claim pays.
- **Cost vs cover** — stepped/level premiums and waiting periods change long-run cost; balance affordability and protection.
- **Personal advice** — sums insured, structure and provider should be set by a personal needs analysis; obtain personal advice and the relevant PDS/TMD before acting.

---

## 15. Glossary

- **Life (death) cover** — pays a lump sum on death or terminal illness.
- **TPD** — total and permanent disability cover; "own" vs "any" occupation definitions differ.
- **Income protection (IP)** — replaces part of your income during illness/injury; waiting and benefit periods apply.
- **Trauma / critical illness** — lump sum on diagnosis of a defined condition.
- **Stepped / level premiums** — premium structures that rise quickly with age vs more slowly.
- **Underwriting** — the insurer's assessment of your health/risk when cover starts.
- **Waiting / benefit period** — the delay before IP pays, and how long it pays for.

## 16. Legislation & rules referenced
